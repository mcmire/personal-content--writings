## The good

I am trying to look at this objectively. Before I dig into AI, I want to spend some time giving credit where it's due.

I consider myself a technologist. I believe technology is an insanely powerful force — a "lever on the world", as Marc Andreesen and others have put it — which allows humans to do _more with less_. And I believe the primary purpose of technology is to %%advance civilization%% "lift up" society by giving people happier, healthier, and more fulfilling lives. 

Where does artificial intelligence fit into this framework? Fundamentally, I view AI as an implementation of _automation_: a machine taking on the responsibility of some portion of a human's job. 

In this sense, AI is very useful. There are many aspects of people's jobs — or whole categories of jobs altogether — that are, quite simply, soul-sucking. For instance, I doubt that janitors at theme parks enjoy cleaning bathrooms, workers at fast-food establishments enjoy dumping questionable frozen substances into burning hot oil, or representatives behind grey cubicles in nameless call centers enjoy being screamed at by angry customers. If you could wave a magic wand so that computers and robots took care of the drudgery of life so that people could do things they actually _wanted_ to do, would you? It certainly seems tempting.
